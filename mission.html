Mission

Technology policy matters too much to leave to adversaries.

For most of the last forty years, the people who write policy and the people who build technology shared an instinct: that technology could make government work better and keep the country ahead. That alliance came apart over the last several years, less because of any single policy than because of how the political class came to treat the industry. Experience building things became a political liability, and officials grew wary of even meeting with tech leaders. Tech started to feel it had stopped being heard.

But that’s no longer a tenable position to be in. Technology is now the foundation under every industry, which raises both the cost of getting this relationship wrong and the urgency of getting it right. Lawmakers and the tech industry still want many of the same things and still need each other: policymakers have gotten better at naming what’s broken than at fixing it, and tech is good at actually building the fixes.

The goal is a relationship where government backs innovation again and tech has an ally instead of an adversary. Bringing tech leaders and policymakers back into the same room is what the Open Progress Institute is for. We put founders, executives, and investors together with the officials and senior staff who write the rules the industry lives by. Those rooms build relationships between people who would otherwise never meet. Where it’s useful, we also commission research and plain-language briefings, then carry what comes out to the people whose decisions it should inform.
